# 03. The 2026 NMJL Card

What the card is, how to read it, what is special about 2026, and how its hands get into the app. This file doesn't list the 2026 hands yet. The full hand list will be entered from your own card (see section 7), because a genuine card is the accurate source: online transcriptions are unofficial and error-prone, and one wrong tile breaks the win checker. Confidence tags: [H] high, [M] medium, [L] low (defined in 01-rules).

---

## 1. Basics

- The NMJL publishes a new card of "Standard Hands and Rules" every year. All play that year is against that card, and last year's hands stop counting. [H] ([NMJL, The Game](https://www.nationalmahjonggleague.org/game.aspx))
- 2026 prices from the NMJL store: standard print $14, large print $15. The NMJL Online app requires owning the card plus a paid membership. [H] ([NMJL store](https://www.nationalmahjonggleague.org/store.aspx))
- The 2026 card reached players early: Tom Sloper's column of March 22, 2026 says it arrived "almost two weeks early," and The Mah Jongg Insider reported cards in mailboxes on March 23. Cards are normally mailed in March and April. [H] ([Sloper column 815](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column2/column815.html); [The Mah Jongg Insider, March 23, 2026](https://mahjong.beehiiv.com/p/03-23-26-the-2026-card-is-hitting-mailboxes-now); [Sloper cards FAQ](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/cards.html))
- Buyers who order directly from the League also get its annual bulletin (mailed each January), which carries rule clarifications. [M] ([Sloper 2026 card FAQ](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/2026.html))
- The back of the card prints rules. For 2026 the claiming and joker rules were revised and the scoring section was removed (details in 01-rules, section 15). [H] ([Sloper column 816](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column2/column816.html))

## 2. Sections (categories) on the 2026 card

In card order [H] (Sloper 2026 card FAQ; [Sweet Jojo Mahjong](https://sweetjojomahjong.com/blogs/news/national-mahjong-league-card-explained-how-to-read-the-2026-nmjl-card); a retailer blog, Spools, lists the same sections in a different order and gets the release date wrong, so it isn't relied on here):

1. **2026** (the "year" hands)
2. **2468** (even numbers)
3. **Any Like Numbers** (the same number across suits)
4. **Quints** (groups of five; always need jokers except flower quints)
5. **Consecutive Run** (numbers in sequence)
6. **13579** (odd numbers)
7. **Winds-Dragons**
8. **369**
9. **Singles and Pairs** (all concealed, no jokers)

The nine-section structure is the same as 2025. [H] ([The Mah Jongg Insider, March 30, 2026](https://mahjong.beehiiv.com/p/03-30-26-before-you-study-the-2026-card-read-this)) For comparison, 2025 dropped the "Addition" and "Multiplication" sections that some earlier cards had. [M] ([I Love Mahj, 2025 analysis](https://ilovemahj.com/2025-card-analysis))

## 3. How many hands, and what they are worth

- **Number of hands: the count depends on how lettered and "-or-" variants are counted.** Reported figures: 55 lines and 1,059 distinct winning hands ([Mahjong Mate listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6478509695)); 65 lines and **1,077 distinct concrete hands** in a careful simulation transcription ([Sarah Gilligan, Fuzzy Match, Oct. 20, 2026](https://fuzzymatch.substack.com/p/i-taught-a-computer-to-play-the-2026)); 72 printed entries counting lettered versions separately, again 1,077 concrete hands ([EightBam](https://eightbam.com/best-sections/), via [Missy Mahjong](https://www.missymahjong.com/post/nmjl-2026-card-statistics)); about 71 to 72 in a teacher's guide ([Mahj Life primer, April 6, 2026](https://mahjlife.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/American-Mah-Jongg-Primer-Companion-20260406.1.pdf)). [M] For reference, 2025 had 71 printed hands and about 1,002 concrete variations ([I Love Mahj, 2025 analysis](https://ilovemahj.com/2025-card-analysis)). **Your card settles the line count**; the concrete-hand count is the check on data entry (section 7).
- **Concrete hands by section** (EightBam's count, via Missy Mahjong) [M]: 2026 (year) 30; 2468 66; Any Like Numbers 135; Quints 264; Consecutive Run 315; 13579 81; Winds-Dragons 107; 369 48; Singles and Pairs 31. Total 1,077.
- **Point values** reported for 2026 [M] (Mahj Life primer):

| Value | Typical use on the 2026 card |
|---|---|
| 25 | easiest exposed hands |
| 30 | many exposed hands and many concealed hands |
| 35 | some concealed hands (35 is back for 2026; 2025 had none) |
| 40, 45 | Quints |
| 50, 75 | Singles and Pairs (75 is the top hand on the card) |

- Points do not always track difficulty; some concealed 30-point hands are notably hard. [M] (Mahj Life; I Love Mahj 2025)

## 4. How to read a card line (this is tutorial content)

Each line is a row of tile "words" separated by spaces. The length of each word tells you the group size. Notation [H unless noted] (Sloper 2026 card FAQ; [Sloper FAQ 19](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq19.html); Sweet Jojo; I Love Mahj guide):

- **Digits** are number tiles. A repeated digit shows group size: pair (2 tiles), pung (3), kong (4), quint (5), sextet (6). Several different digits in one word (like a run) are single tiles.
- **F** = Flower. **D** = Dragon. **N, E, W, S** = Winds. **0 = Soap** (the White Dragon) in every year hand, whatever color it is printed in. Zero is suitless.
- **Colors show how many suits a hand uses, not which suits.** Same color = same suit; different colors = different suits. The player chooses the actual suits. A one-color hand is one suit; three colors means all three suits. Sloper notes that suitless tiles (flowers, winds, zeros) are printed in blue, which should not be read as a suit. [H for the color rule; M for the blue detail]
- **Parentheses override the colors.** If the small print says "any 2 suits" or "any dragon," that wins. [H] (Sloper FAQ 19)
- **X or C** at the right edge: X = exposures allowed; C = concealed. The value is printed beside it. (Note: at least one consumer website gets X and C backwards; trust the card.)
- **"Any like numbers"** means the same number in each group.
- **Consecutive** runs must keep the order shown on the card. [H]
- **Dragons go with suits**: Red with Craks, Green with Bams, White (Soap) with Dots. "Matching" dragons share the suit of the numbers next to them; "opposite" dragons are a different suit; "any dragon" is any. [H] (Sloper 2026 card FAQ)
- **Winds and numbers**: by long-standing card convention, East and West go with even numbers and North and South with odd numbers (Sloper's mnemonic: "East equals Even"). What's new in 2026 is that two hands use it (an E-W hand in 2468 and an N-S hand in 13579). The winds in each hand are printed or fixed by the small print, so the player never picks winds by odd or even; the card data should record winds exactly as printed. [H] (Sloper 2026 card FAQ: "the tradition on the NMJL card"; Sloper column 815)
- **Jokers** are never used for a single tile or in a pair; only in groups of 3 or more identical tiles. Groupings such as NEWS and 2026 are singles, so they take no jokers and can never be claimed except as the Mah Jongg tile. [H]

## 5. What is new or tricky on the 2026 card

All described in general terms. [M unless noted] (Sloper column 815; Sloper 2026 card FAQ; Mahj Life primer; [I Love Mahj 2026 clarifications](https://ilovemahj.com/kb/67000752308); The Mah Jongg Insider)

- **Flowers matter more.** Two hands need a sextet of six flowers, and two others need two separate flower pungs; there are no flower kongs this year. About nine hands need three or more flowers, so expect competition for flowers. A flower sextet is "unbreakable" (exposed as one sextet); the two-pung hands' pungs are "exposable at separate times." [H for these structural facts]
- **Wind pairs by number.** A new E-W hand appears in 2468, and an N-S hand in 13579.
- **Runs.** Consecutive Run includes a hand with a three-number run (a "chow") nested between other groups; Winds-Dragons includes a four-number run.
- **All three dragons.** Three hands use all three dragons: two use them as three single tiles (Any Like Numbers and Singles and Pairs), and one uses them as two pungs and a kong (Winds-Dragons). [H] (Sloper column 815, rechecked)
- **Most common shape**: pung, pung, kong, kong (nine hands). Sloper calls one Consecutive Run hand of this shape the most powerful hand on the card, with "cousin" hands of the same shape in 2468, 13579, and 369.
- **Floating pairs**: some 13579 and 369 hands let any one of the numbers be the pair, with the kongs required to match the pair's number.
- **Restricted numbers**: some hands allow only specific numbers for a group (for example a kong that may be one of two named numbers), and some allow only the exact numbers printed.
- **Carry-overs**: about five hands return from 2025 (Mahj Life). Several returning patterns have been regrouped; for example, last year's version of the top run hand was pung-kong-pung-kong, and this year's is pung-pung-kong-kong. Read familiar-looking lines carefully. [M-H] (Sloper column 815; The Mah Jongg Insider, March 30 and April 13, 2026)
- **Two same-number pungs exposed = dead hand.** On the 2026 card, only concealed hands use two pungs of the same number, so a player who exposes two such pungs fits no legal hand and can be called dead (Sloper calls these exposures "a dead giveaway"). The engine catches this automatically; the coach can teach it as a defensive point. [H] (Sloper column 815; dead-hand rule 15(f) via Sloper FAQ 19)

## 6. What simulation says about the 2026 card (useful for coaching and bots)

From Sarah Gilligan's October 2026 study: a four-player engine with three bot skill levels played 6,000 games on the 2026 card. [M] (single careful source; the author notes results depend on her transcription and simple bots)

- A random 13-tile rack starts about **7 tiles away** from its closest hand; fewer than 3% start within 5.
- **Consecutive Run** hands won about **30%** of games. **Quints** won almost never (12 of 5,328 wins).
- About **67%** of wins came on another player's discard; about **11%** of games were wall games; a game averaged about **67 discards**.
- East won 24.5% of games versus about 21.7% for each other seat.
- Winning hands used about 2 jokers on average; jokerless wins were about 3%.
- The biggest skill gap was **committing to one hand too early** (the "beginner" bot). Counting live tiles and light defense helped less than expected.

A second, larger simulation agrees on the big picture: EightBam ran 10 million games on the 2026 card (without bot strategy, exploring both paths whenever a discard could be called) and reports Consecutive Run hands "showing up" in 46.9% of games versus 0.04% for Quints. [M] ([EightBam, best sections](https://eightbam.com/best-sections/); [EightBam, probability](https://eightbam.com/probability/); methodology only partly described)

**Takeaway for coaching**: point beginners toward Consecutive Run and other flexible sections, and away from Quints, which look tempting because they are worth more.

## 7. Getting the hands into the app (plan for the build chat)

1. **Photograph the card** when it arrives: front and back, flat, good light, each panel readable. Large print is fine.
2. **Transcribe each line into the card data format** described in 07-app-spec: groups, suit variables, number variables, dragon relations, X/C, value, and any parenthetical constraints. Keep the card's section and line numbers as IDs (for example "2468-3") so the app can say "you are going for 2468 #3."
3. **Expand** every line into all its concrete hands automatically. First define what counts as one hand (suggested: a distinct set of 14 natural tiles after removing suit swaps that produce the same tiles). Then compare the total with the roughly 1,077 that Gilligan and EightBam report, as a soft cross-check only, since neither defines its counting unit. The real gate is steps 4 and 5.
4. **Verify line by line**: the app shows one sample concrete hand per line next to your photo, and you confirm each one. This catches color and parenthetical misreadings, which teachers say are the most common errors.
5. **Test**: for every line, build a winning hand and confirm the checker accepts it, then break it one tile at a time and confirm it is rejected.
6. **Store the hands as their own data file** that ships with the app, so next year's card is a drop-in replacement. The cheat sheet uses the app's own layout and colors (see 08-design-ideas).
7. **Until the card arrives**, the build chat should use a small invented practice card (clearly fake hands covering every pattern type) so the engine, tutorial, and tests can be built first.

## 8. Legal position (settled)

- **The hands themselves are unprotectable game rules.** This is the project's settled position, set by you as an attorney, for this personal, non-commercial app. The hand list can be stored and used freely inside the app; the card data needs no special handling.
- The app still uses its own tile art and card layout. That is a design choice (you wanted custom tiles and a custom card look), and it keeps the app clear of the printed card's own design.
- Background reading that reaches the same conclusion about the hands: [Lerner David (July 2026)](https://www.lernerdavid.com/news-successes/unscripted/copyright-issues-in-america-s-fast-growing-mah-jongg-craze) and [TMBTQ (May 2025)](https://www.tmbtq.com/post/ip-and-mah-jongg/).
- **Make sure your card is genuine.** Counterfeit cards with wrong hands are sold on Amazon, Temu, and eBay; buy from the NMJL or an authorized seller. The League also issues corrections: the 2025 card had color printing errors on at least two lines and was reprinted. Check the League's January bulletin for 2026 clarifications.
