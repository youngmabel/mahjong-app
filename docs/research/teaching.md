# Teaching American Mah Jongg to Beginners

> **Note added after the independent fact check (research/factcheck.md):** where this file and the main notes (01 to 08) differ, the main notes carry the corrected version.

Research notes for the tutorial, guided matches and graduation design of a single-player NMJL practice app (one human against three bots, 2026 NMJL card, standard 152-tile set with no blanks). Compiled October 26, 2026.

## How to read these notes

Sources are cited inline, with a consolidated list at the end. Key claims carry a confidence tag (High, Medium or Low) with a short reason. "Official" means a National Mah Jongg League (NMJL) rule as relayed by the League's card, its website, or Tom Sloper's rules FAQs, which cite the League's rulebook *Mah Jongg Made Easy* ([Sloper FAQ 3](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq03.html)). I could not read the rulebook itself, so every rule detail below should be checked against it before it is encoded in the app. "House rule" means a table custom, a tournament or online-platform rule, or a simplification a teacher uses only while teaching. No hands from the NMJL card are reproduced here.

The research pass hit its web-search limit near the end. Gaps that remain because of that are listed in the last section.

## 1. How teachers and guides sequence beginner lessons

### 1.2 Where sources agree

Every source starts with the tiles. Nearly every modern class teaches the card before full games and the Charleston after the card, because Charleston passing depends on knowing which hands you might play. Calling and exposures are taught during live play rather than in the abstract. Strategy, scoring and etiquette come last. Typical courses run 2 to 5 sessions, and Sloper says "I always recommend a minimum of 3 lessons" and that "Most students want a 4th lesson" ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html), 2012). Confidence: High for tiles first and strategy last (every source agrees); Medium-High for card before Charleston (most agree, but Sloper introduces both in the same lesson).

### 1.3 Where sources disagree

**Card first or play first.** Sandberg will not hand a student 13 or 14 tiles at the start, calling that "working backward"; she teaches the card's hands first ([column 308](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column308a.html)). Sloper does the opposite: students play simple four-pungs-and-a-pair hands before they see the card, because "I want them to have fun before I hit'em with the hard stuff" ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html), 2012). Lynn M and Miss Mahjong also play before the card. Most commercial classes side with Sandberg. Both camps use play to teach the turn and the card to teach the goal. They differ only on which comes first.

**Which card section first.** Sloper starts at the top left of the card with the year hands ("I start at the upper left corner and explain the 2011 hands first") and holds Consecutive Runs until after Quints, because runs give "the slower players the most trouble" ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html), 2011). Donna Miller-Small does hand-building drills starting with the Consecutive Run section, which she calls the most important because it allows the most variations ([Mahjong Line](https://themahjongline.com/blogs/mahjong-tales/donna-miller-small-the-marvelous-mrs-mah-jongg-maven)). These views fit together: runs are hard to read and also worth the most practice.

**When jokers arrive.** Lynn M brings jokers in at session 1. Santa Barbara teaches joker basics alongside the card on day 2. Sloper argues that teaching with jokers is harder because their rules must be explained too, and calls the joker rules "rather complex" ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html), 2023). Miss Mahjong lets children use jokers anywhere at first and adds the official restrictions later. That is a teaching simplification, not an NMJL rule.

**Walls.** Sloper skips them at first ("Wall-building takes too much time and isn't fun"): students pick from a face-down pile until they have played a little ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html), 2012). Lexington teaches walls in its first sessions. For an app this matters little, because the software builds the wall.

**Coaching during play.** In lessons, Sloper and others advise players live while tiles are face up. In social play with a novice, Sloper says to keep strategy advice to yourself during the game; defensive play, for instance, "you could do that between games" ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html), 2010).

**Chinese rules first.** Sloper's general beginner FAQ suggests learning simplified Chinese rules before American ([FAQ 10](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq10.html)). No American-focused teacher found here does this, and it does not suit an NMJL-only app. Its useful idea, leaving out flowers, jokers and kongs at first, is the same "simple hands first" idea as above.

## 2. What beginners find hardest, and the most common mistakes

### 2.1 The overall picture

Sloper is blunt: "The card IS the most difficult thing to teach (and the most difficult thing for an NMJL player to learn)" ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html), 2011). When a reader said that choosing a hand and selecting tiles to pass is the hardest part, his reply began "Absolutely!" (2012). I Love Mahj also calls choosing a hand one of the hardest tasks for beginners ([St. Louis Jewish Light, 2021](https://stljewishlight.org/news/news-local/this-week-in-mah-jongg-lets-play)). After that come the joker rules ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html), 2023), slow play ([column 375](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column375a.html)), and vocabulary that feels like a foreign language ([Anders Ruff](https://andersruff.com/custom-printable-parties/?p=76036)). For scale, one app counts 55 lines and 1,059 distinct winning hands on the 2026 card ([Mahjong Mate listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6478509695); a third-party count, not checked). Memorizing the card is hopeless; learners need to read families and notation. Mahjong 4 Friends lists "trying to memorize the card instead of focusing on categories" as a beginner mistake ([guide](https://mahjong4friends.com/guides/mah-jongg-for-beginners)). Confidence: High that card reading and hand selection are the central difficulty; several independent teachers agree, and courses give the card the most time (Barnett spends three chapters on it).

### 2.2 Reading the card: concept by concept

| Concept | What beginners get wrong | The correct reading | Teaching move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Colors | Assume the color names the suit, for example green means Bams. | Colors show how many suits a hand uses and which tiles share a suit. Any suit can take any color unless a note says otherwise. Suitless tiles (flowers, winds, zeros) are printed in blue or black, and blue does not mean "any suit." ([Sloper 2026 FAQ](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/2026.html); [I Love Mahj](https://ilovemahj.com/american-mahjong-getting-started)) Mahjong 4 Friends lists the color-equals-suit assumption as a top mistake ([guide](https://mahjong4friends.com/guides/mah-jongg-for-beginners)). Sloper flags it in recent beginner books ([column 817](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column2/column817.html)). | Show one line built correctly in several suit combinations, all accepted. |
| Any like numbers | Read the printed 1s literally. | "Like" means the same number; the printed number stands for whichever number you choose ([Sloper 2026 FAQ](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/2026.html); [I Love Mahj, 2023](https://stljewishlight.org/arts-entertainment/the-new-2023-mah-jongg-card)). | Have the learner build the same line with 3s, then 7s. |
| Consecutive runs | Treat runs as Chinese-style chows (claimable, joker-friendly groups), change the order, or put zeros in them. | Runs printed from 1 can start at another number unless a note such as "these numbers only" limits them. Numbers must keep the order shown, and zeros appear only where the card prints a 0. A run is a set of single tiles: "There is no such thing as 'chowing' in this game," jokers cannot be used in it, and a discard can complete it only for Mah Jongg ([Sloper 2026 FAQ](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/2026.html); [I Love Mahj, 2023](https://stljewishlight.org/arts-entertainment/the-new-2023-mah-jongg-card)). Recent beginner books get this wrong ([column 817](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column2/column817.html)). The word still turns up in teaching talk: Sloper's first look at the 2026 card loosely calls a three-number run a "chow" and a four-number run a "rummy run" ([column 815](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column2/column815.html)). | Teach after like numbers. Use "slide the run" drills, and show that a run cannot be called or filled with a joker. |
| Matching and opposite dragons | Treat dragons as free tiles and miscount the suits. | Red goes with Craks, Green with Bams, and White (Soap) with Dots. A dragon printed in a number group's color must match that group's suit. A dragon printed in a color the numbers do not use belongs to a suit the numbers do not use (an "opposite" dragon). Wording such as "Any 2 suits" or "Any 3 suits" changes how dragons count. ([I Love Mahj, 2023](https://stljewishlight.org/arts-entertainment/the-new-2023-mah-jongg-card); [Mah Jongg Insider](https://mahjong.beehiiv.com/p/0202klp8); [Sloper 2026 FAQ](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/2026.html)) Sloper's FAQ does not cleanly define "opposite," so let each line's own note decide. | "Count the suits" puzzles in the multiple-choice style the Insider uses. |
| NEWS and the year group | Treat NEWS or "2026" as a kong, use jokers in it, or call a tile for it. | Both are groups of single tiles, not kongs. Jokers are not allowed in them, and you cannot call a discard for them except to win. ([Sloper 2026 FAQ](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/2026.html); [I Love Mahj](https://ilovemahj.com/american-mahjong-getting-started)) | Block with a short explanation the first time it happens. |
| White Dragon (Soap) as zero | Use any white-looking tile, or use the zero where the card has no 0. | Soap is the only tile used as zero, whatever color the 0 is printed in. Zero is suitless and is used only where a 0 is printed. ([Sloper 2026 FAQ](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/2026.html); [Forsyth Tech's rule of thumb "zero = Soap"](https://www.forsythtech.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/MahjongSupplies-vFall26.pdf)) | Introduce Soap's double role (a dragon that also serves as zero) in the tile lesson, then reinforce it on the card. |
| X and C | Do not realize that C forbids calling. | X marks a hand that may call and expose. C marks a concealed hand that may call only the final, winning tile; exposing any group kills it. Jokers are still allowed inside concealed sets. ([I Love Mahj](https://ilovemahj.com/american-mahjong-getting-started); [Sloper FAQ 19](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq19.html)) | Grey out the call button, with a reason, while the pinned hand is a C hand. |
| Point values | Think the printed value is the final score. | The printed value is the base. The back of the card says the discarder of the winning tile pays double, everyone pays double on a self-drawn win, and a jokerless hand doubles. Sloper reads these doubles as stacking ([column 316](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column316a.html)). Hands that cannot use jokers get no jokerless double, because it is already built into their value ([Sloper 2026 FAQ](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/2026.html)). | Teach late, in an end-of-game summary. |
| Notes in parentheses | Skip them. | Notes override the defaults, for example limiting a run to the numbers shown or freeing the suits ([I Love Mahj, 2023](https://stljewishlight.org/arts-entertainment/the-new-2023-mah-jongg-card)). Miller-Small's tip: read the fine print ([Mahjong Line](https://themahjongline.com/blogs/mahjong-tales/donna-miller-small-the-marvelous-mrs-mah-jongg-maven)). | Highlight the note whenever it changes the answer. |

Sloper's 2024 "quandaries" column shows the errors real players make: declaring a hand that is not on the card, thinking jokers are banned only in the year group, building two suits when three are required, using pungs where the card shows pairs, mixing numbers in pungs that must match, and playing a hand from the previous year's card ([column 809](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column2/column809.html)). On the 2026 card, Sloper flags flower sextets, concealed-only hands, and "cousin" hands in different sections that look alike once two exposures are down, which he notes can fool players ([column 815](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column2/column815.html)). Confidence: High for the correct readings, which come from the NMJL's conventions as several sources relay them; Medium for "opposite dragons," whose wording varies from source to source.

### 2.3 Jokers

Official rules as relayed by Sloper and I Love Mahj: jokers count only in groups of three or more identical tiles (pungs, kongs, quints, sextets). They never count in singles, pairs, NEWS, year groups, runs, or the Singles and Pairs hands. They cannot be passed in the Charleston. A discarded joker is dead and cannot be called ("Down is dead"). On your own turn you may exchange a matching natural tile for a joker in any exposure, including your own, and you may make several exchanges in one turn. You cannot call a discard just to redeem a joker. ([Sloper FAQ 19](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq19.html); [Sloper 2026 FAQ](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/2026.html); [I Love Mahj guide](https://ilovemahj.com/american-mahjong-getting-started); [I Love Mahj, 2021](https://stljewishlight.org/news/news-local/this-week-in-mah-jongg-lets-play)) Typical beginner errors include using jokers in pairs ([Mahjong 4 Friends](https://mahjong4friends.com/guides/mah-jongg-for-beginners); [Miller-Small](https://themahjongline.com/blogs/mahjong-tales/donna-miller-small-the-marvelous-mrs-mah-jongg-maven)), believing jokers are banned only in the year group ([column 809](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column2/column809.html)), and trying to redeem a joker before calling ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html)). Miller-Small adds a strategy point: pairs are the hard part of a hand because jokers cannot fill them and they cannot be called, so a beginner holding jokers but chasing a pairs-heavy hand ends up discarding jokers. Confidence: High; these rules are consistent across sources.

### 2.4 Calling and exposing

Official rules as relayed by Sloper: you may claim a discard only to complete a pung, kong, quint or sextet, or to win. A single or pair may be claimed only when it is your winning tile, and never when the discard is a joker. Only the most recent discard can be claimed. The window opens when the tile is down or named and closes when the next player racks, discards, declares, exchanges a joker or starts an exposure. A Mah Jongg claim beats an exposure claim; between two exposure claims, the player next in turn wins, unless the other claimant has already begun exposing. Exposed groups need not be in card order during play, but the winning hand is laid out in card order. ([Sloper FAQ 19](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq19.html); consistent with [I Love Mahj](https://ilovemahj.com/american-mahjong-getting-started) and [Mahjong Line](https://themahjongline.com/pages/the-big-card-american-mahjong-101)) Common errors: exposing more than the completed group, exposing a concealed hand, and redeeming a joker before calling ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html)). Another is exposing early and so revealing your hand ([Mahjong 4 Friends](https://mahjong4friends.com/guides/mah-jongg-for-beginners)). Miller-Small says to rack a drawn tile right away, because fumbling with it can cost you when someone else calls in time ([Mahjong Line](https://themahjongline.com/blogs/mahjong-tales/donna-miller-small-the-marvelous-mrs-mah-jongg-maven)).

Sources disagree on one point: whether a player can resize or correct an exposure after placing it. The Mahjong Line allows changing its size until you discard or exchange a joker, I Love Mahj allows edits until the discard, and the Forsyth outline says the exposure "can't be changed" once the tile is called. Verify this in *Mah Jongg Made Easy* before coding it. Confidence: High for the core calling rules; Low for the exposure-correction detail.

Dead hands are the main consequence of misreading. Sloper's list of causes includes declaring a hand that is not on the card, holding a hand that can no longer be completed, exposing a concealed hand, a wrong tile count, playing out of turn and an incorrect exposure ([FAQ 19](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq19.html)). In tournaments and on online platforms, wrongly calling someone else dead can kill your own hand ([I Love Mahj](https://ilovemahj.com/american-mahjong-getting-started)). That is a platform or tournament rule, not a universal home rule. Sloper's 2006 column describes a group of newcomers whose argument came from a wrongful dead call and muddled house rules, and he advises learning the official rules first so you can "break them properly" ([column 291](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column291a.html)).

### 2.5 The Charleston and hand selection

Mechanics (official): a required first Charleston of three passes, right, across, left; an optional second Charleston, left, across, right, which any player may stop after the first; blind passing allowed only on the first left and the last right, where you may pass along up to three tiles you were just handed without looking at them; then an optional courtesy pass of 0 to 3 tiles with the player across, using whichever number is lower. Jokers are never passed. ([I Love Mahj on blind passes](https://ilovemahj.com/mah-jongg-question/606/what-is-a-blind-pass-and-when-can-i-use-it); [I Love Mahj on stopping](https://ilovemahj.com/mah-jongg-question/601/when-can-you-stop-passing-during-the-charleston-in-mah-jongg); [Mahjong Line](https://themahjongline.com/pages/the-big-card-american-mahjong-101)) Teachers use the mnemonic "ROLLOR" (Right, Over, Left, Left, Over, Right) ([Mahjong 4 Friends](https://mahjong4friends.com/guides/mah-jongg-for-beginners); [Forsyth Tech](https://www.forsythtech.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/MahjongSupplies-vFall26.pdf)). A blind pass is sometimes called "stealing," and one source notes that beginners misread "blind" as meaning they cannot look at their own tiles ([I Love Mahj](https://ilovemahj.com/mah-jongg-question/606/what-is-a-blind-pass-and-when-can-i-use-it)).

Strategy is where beginners struggle. Sloper says beginners wrongly believe the Charleston is about finding "the one correct hand to go for," when its real job is to choose a loose direction and pass away what does not fit ([column 630](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column2/column630.html)). His updated method has five steps ([column 725](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column2/column725.html), 2019): (0) count your jokers (none suggests Singles and Pairs; two or more suggests Quints); (1) find your pairs, pungs and grouped singles; (2) find hands where those pairs work together ("friends"); (3) if that fails, compare low, middle and high numbers, with runs in mind; (4) if that fails, compare odd and even. Stop as soon as a step points to a hand, and keep moving, because the Charleston "needs to move quickly." Mahjong 4 Friends lists locking into a hand before the Charleston ends, and passing honors and flowers too quickly, as beginner mistakes, and recommends narrowing to two candidate hands ([guide](https://mahjong4friends.com/guides/mah-jongg-for-beginners)). Sloper's "Charleston quandary" columns walk through real racks and explain each pass, which makes them ready-made worked examples ([column 779](https://www.sloperama.com/mahjongg/column2/column779.html); [column 729](https://www.sloperama.com/mahjongg/column2/column729.html)). Confidence: High that hand selection in the Charleston is a major difficulty; Medium that Sloper's step method is the best framework to teach (one expert's method, but a clear and well-tested one).

### 2.6 Pace and table habits

Slow play is the problem most often raised about beginners after they leave class. Sloper's advice to a group with a slow player was "Keep the game moving!" He noted that the common beginner problem is agonizing over defense instead of building a hand, and that "We were all slow once" ([column 375](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column375a.html)). He calls a teacher's "10-second rule" for Charleston passes "not unreasonable" and allows 15 seconds, while treating the limit as a group decision ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html), 2023). Table habits differ, for example how quickly players pick and rack, and newcomers need to learn each table's customs ([column 480](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column480a.html)). The 10 to 15 second figure is a table custom, not an official rule.

### 2.7 A content-quality warning

In May 2026 Sloper reviewed several new beginner books he suspects were AI-written. They contain errors that match the misconceptions above: claiming the card specifies suits, treating runs as chows, recommending chows, letting pungs be exposed freely while only kongs must be concealed, inventing four wind rounds, and getting the direction of play or the deal wrong ([column 817](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column2/column817.html)). The app's own tutorial text should be checked against that list. One beginner-mistakes article found in this research ([themahjong.com](https://themahjong.com/blog/mahjong-beginner-mistakes)) describes the blind pass in a way that conflicts with the I Love Mahj and Mahjong Line descriptions, so it is not used as evidence here.

## 3. Teaching aids that work

**Cheat sheets.** Teachers and guides advise keeping a one-page reference within reach during play, and say you do not need every rule before your first game ([I Love Mahj, 2021](https://stljewishlight.org/news/news-local/this-week-in-mah-jongg-lets-play); [Anders Ruff](https://andersruff.com/custom-printable-parties/?p=76036)). The cheat sheets reviewed cover the card's sections, its symbols (X, C, letters for winds, dragons and flowers), what the colors mean, an "if you have this, look at that family" guide to choosing a hand, and Charleston tips ([Mahjong 4 Friends cheat sheet](https://mahjong4friends.com/cms-assets/Nmjl%20Card%20&%20Hand%20Selection%20Quick%20Reference%20Cheat%20Sheet.pdf)). The Forsyth outline compresses the card into rules of thumb: a color change means a suit change, zero means Soap, X is exposed and C is concealed ([Forsyth Tech](https://www.forsythtech.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/MahjongSupplies-vFall26.pdf)). I Love Mahj also publishes a printable cheat sheet ([Learn page](https://ilovemahj.com/learn-american-mah-jongg)).

**Card-reading guides and quizzes.** Most books build in checks: Sandberg's quizzes and practice card ([column 308](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column308a.html)), Barnett's quiz with an answer key after every chapter ([listing](https://www.foxtalebookshoppe.com/book/9781733168007)), and Swain and Salk's exercises, quizzes and paper tiles ([listing](https://themahjongline.com/products/american-mah-jongg-for-everyone)). The Mah Jongg Insider newsletter poses multiple-choice questions ("which of these racks satisfies this line?") whose wrong options are built on miscounted suits and mismatched dragons ([Insider](https://mahjong.beehiiv.com/p/0202klp8)).

**Practice drills.** I Love Mahj's Exercise Room offers three kinds of drill: make a hand from tiles, identify possible hands from a few tiles, and work out an opponent's hand from their exposures ([Learn page](https://ilovemahj.com/learn-american-mah-jongg)). Miller-Small's drill is to sort tiles by type, pick one section of the card, and build every hand in it with real tiles ([Mahjong Line](https://themahjongline.com/blogs/mahjong-tales/donna-miller-small-the-marvelous-mrs-mah-jongg-maven)). Sloper's Big Square is a tile-naming drill: students pick up tiles as he names them ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html)). Sloper's Charleston quandaries work as decision puzzles ([column 779](https://www.sloperama.com/mahjongg/column2/column779.html)). Saying each discard's name aloud, which is table etiquette, also drills tile names for everyone ([Mahjong Line field guide](https://themahjongline.com/cdn/shop/files/Mahjong_Field_Guide_Mountain_Sky_Blue.pdf)).

**Open-hand practice games.** Playing with racks face up is the standard way to run early games. Sloper's lesson 3 is played "at one table, with their tiles all exposed, so I can advise," and students conceal their tiles only once they stop struggling ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html)). Lynn M runs one table with hands exposed so everyone can hear, and Miss Mahjong plays face up until children recognize the cues on their own. General board-game teaching reaches the same conclusion: games with hidden information usually have to be played openly the first time ([the "Paul Grogan method," as described by a tabletop blog](https://tabletopgamesblog.com/2019/04/16/teacher-teacher)). Confidence: High that open-hand first games are standard teaching practice.

**Coached games.** Formats include a coach-led first game followed by a game on the student's own with one-on-one coaching ([Menage a Mahj](https://www.tickettailor.com/events/menageamahj/2368228)), experienced club players sitting in with students ([Lynn M in FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html)), guided play as the final class session ([Santa Barbara](https://www.jewishsantabarbara.org/events/mah-jongg-beginner-class-series/); [Lexington](https://lexingtoncommunityed.org/?p=74567)), and online Zoom practice sessions offered by the American Mah Jongg Association ([Insider](https://mahjong.beehiiv.com/p/0202klp8)).

**How teachers run a first game.** Putting the sources together, the typical first game looks like this. It is announced as a practice game that does not count, and mistakes are expected ([Anders Ruff](https://andersruff.com/custom-printable-parties/?p=76036)). Tiles go into learners' hands quickly rather than after a long explanation. Racks are face up so the teacher can advise. The target is a simple hand (four pungs and a pair) or a single card section, and walls may be skipped ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html)). Rules are explained when the situation comes up, "layer the complexities on bit by bit" (Sloper, 2012). The Charleston is walked through slowly and the teacher explains why each move works. The teacher may take the first turn while narrating the choices ([Grogan method](https://tabletopgamesblog.com/2019/04/16/teacher-teacher)). Tiles are concealed only when students are ready. Questions are explicitly welcome ([Anders Ruff](https://andersruff.com/custom-printable-parties/?p=76036)). Confidence: Medium-High; the protocol is assembled from several teachers, and its pieces recur across them.

**Vocabulary and naming stories.** Sloper opens with a few stories about the origins of the game during tile identification ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html)), and Judy D, another teacher in the same FAQ, includes history in her course. Name meanings found in this pass: Bams are bamboo, Craks are characters, and Dots are circles ([Forsyth Tech](https://www.forsythtech.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/MahjongSupplies-vFall26.pdf)); the 1 Bam is usually drawn as a bird, and all flowers are interchangeable whatever their pictures ([I Love Mahj](https://ilovemahj.com/american-mahjong-getting-started)); the White Dragon is nicknamed "Soap" ([Mahjong Line](https://themahjongline.com/blogs/mahjong-tales/which-dragons-go-with-which-suits-in-mahjong)), though no source here explains why; "like" numbers means same numbers; a blind pass is also called "stealing"; and a discarded joker is dead ("Down is dead," [FAQ 19](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq19.html)). Other origin stories (the Charleston's name, pung, kong, quint) were not researched here and should come from a sourced history pass.

## 5. Learning design principles and what each means for this app

**Scaffolding, then handing over.** Wood, Bruner and Ross (1976) introduced "scaffolding": tutoring support that lets a novice succeed beyond unaided ability, withdrawn as competence grows. Six tutor functions (recruiting interest, simplifying the task, keeping the learner on track, marking critical features, controlling frustration and demonstrating) are usually credited to that paper, though one recent commentary credits them to a 1978 follow-up study ([Hendrick, 2025](https://carlhendrick.substack.com/p/we-need-to-talk-about-scaffolding); a secondary source, attribution unresolved). *For the app:* simplify early scenarios (fewer tile types, one decision at a time); highlight the critical features, such as the tiles on the rack that match the pinned line; control frustration with practice games that do not count and an undo button; demonstrate with bots that narrate their choices; and plan the removal of every aid from the start.

**One concept per scenario.** Nintendo's level design in Super Mario 3D World introduces one mechanic, develops it, twists it and then concludes, as a "self-contained showcase" for each idea ([MCV, on Game Maker's Toolkit](https://www.mcvuk.com/development/video-nintendos-level-design-secrets-in-four-steps)). Sloper's "layer the complexities on bit by bit" is the same idea in a classroom ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html)). *For the app:* give each tutorial scenario one teaching point and run it through four beats. For jokers: a joker completes a pung (introduce); a joker sits in an exposure (develop); try to put a joker in a pair and see why it fails (twist); redeem a joker from a bot's exposure (conclude).

**Learning by doing, with instructions given in context.** In a test with more than 45,000 players across three games, tutorials helped only in the complex game, Foldit. There, instructions delivered in context, at the moment they applied, produced 40 percent more levels completed than an up-front manual. Restricting what players could do showed no benefit, and on-demand help sometimes lowered engagement ([Andersen et al., CHI 2012](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/chi2012.pdf)). Game design writing makes the same distinction between up-front, demonstration-based, experience-based and just-in-time tutorials ([Khalifa, Game Developer, 2017](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/different-types-of-tutorials)). Gee lists "on demand and just in time" information among the learning principles of good games ([Gee 2004, via Glasgow](https://www.psy.gla.ac.uk/~steve/best/gee.html)). American mah jongg is closer to Foldit than to a casual game, so a tutorial is worth building. That is an inference: no study has tested mah jongg. *For the app:* do not front-load rules. Show each rule card the first time its situation arises (the first callable discard, the first chance to redeem a joker, the first concealed line). Let the learner make the wrong move and then explain, rather than locking out every wrong click. Confidence: Medium (one large study, with effects only in the complex game).

**Simplified models first ("fish tanks") and low-stakes practice ("sandboxes").** Gee recommends simplified systems that highlight a few variables before the full system, and realistic but low-risk spaces for practice ([Gee 2004](https://www.psy.gla.ac.uk/~steve/best/gee.html)). His "well-ordered problems" principle says early solutions should still work on later, harder problems. *For the app:* a "four pungs and a pair" round with no card is a fish tank, matching Sloper's first lesson. Guided matches with face-up racks are sandboxes. The well-ordered principle argues against teaching a temporary rule, such as jokers being allowed anywhere, that the learner must later unlearn.

**Worked examples, then fading.** For novices, studying worked examples beats solving problems unaided. Fading, which gradually removes the worked steps until the learner solves alone, is the recommended path from one to the other ([Renkl and Atkinson, Educational Psychologist, 2003](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/structuring-the-transition-from-example-study-to-problem-solving--2); [overview with citations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worked-example_effect)). As learners gain expertise, the same examples become redundant and can hurt (the expertise-reversal effect; Kalyuga and colleagues, 2003, cited in the same overview). The "Paul Grogan method" of teaching board games applies this informally: the teacher plays the first turn aloud, including the options rejected ([tabletop blog](https://tabletopgamesblog.com/2019/04/16/teacher-teacher)). *For the app:* in the Charleston and hand-selection scenarios, first show a complete narrated decision; then a partly finished one, where the app picks two tiles to pass and the learner picks the third; then the learner decides alone with feedback. Let experienced players test out of the basics. Confidence: High (a robust research literature for well-structured skills).

**Immediate, specific, short feedback for novices, delayed feedback later.** Shute's review recommends immediate feedback, at least at first, for difficult tasks and lower-achieving learners. It recommends delayed feedback for simple tasks and stronger learners, and notes that delay is associated with better transfer. Feedback should say how to improve rather than just "right" or "wrong," be "as simple and focused as possible," come in small pieces, avoid comparisons with other people, and not interrupt a learner deep in a task. The timing evidence is mixed ([Shute 2008, Review of Educational Research](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/shute%202008_b.pdf)). In Koedinger and Aleven's tutor studies, error feedback that explains is better than yes or no feedback ([Koedinger and Aleven 2007](https://pslcdatashop.web.cmu.edu/KDDCup/FAQ/Koedinger-Aleven-EPR-07.pdf)). *For the app:* in the tutorial and the first guided match, correct rule errors immediately in one or two sentences that explain why. Move strategy feedback to an end-of-hand review in later matches, which also mirrors Sloper's "between games" etiquette. Confidence: Medium (sound principles, mixed evidence on timing).

**Hints and the assistance dilemma.** Giving help makes the learner more accurate and faster but can lead to shallow thinking; withholding it encourages effort but risks floundering. In tutor logs, students often skip straight to the "bottom-out" hint that gives the answer, and often fail to ask for help after repeated errors. The paper's tentative answer on whether hints pushed by the system help is "no." Hints are given mainly on request, and a hint request lowers the tutor's estimate of the student's mastery ([Koedinger and Aleven 2007](https://pslcdatashop.web.cmu.edu/KDDCup/FAQ/Koedinger-Aleven-EPR-07.pdf), published in [Educational Psychology Review](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10648-007-9049-0); see also [Miwa et al. 2013](https://repositories.cdlib.org/content/qt9pp919fg/qt9pp919fg.pdf?t=os6029)). *For the app:* tiered hints on request (a nudge, then the family, then the specific line, then the answer), with the final tier unlocked only after an attempt. Count hint use as evidence the skill is not yet mastered. Keep unprompted help rare, since the evidence that it helps is weak; reserve it for repeated errors on one skill, where the authors' rough rule (more help when errors pile up) applies. Confidence: Medium (evidence comes from math and programming tutors).

**Practice testing and spacing.** A major review rated practice testing and spaced (distributed) practice as the two highest-utility study techniques, and rereading and highlighting as low ([Dunlosky et al. 2013, via APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html)). A meta-analysis of 317 experiments found that spaced study improves retention, and that the best gap between study sessions grows with how long the learning must be retained ([Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted and Rohrer 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/)). *For the app:* end each scenario with a two- or three-item retrieval check, the same idea as the quizzes in Barnett's and Sandberg's books. Schedule short card-reading puzzles between sessions at growing intervals, and return to earlier concepts inside later scenarios. Confidence: High.

**Mastery-based progression.** Cognitive Tutors estimate, skill by skill, the probability that a student has learned it. Students keep getting problems targeting their weak skills until every skill passes a threshold "typically set to .95," and this produced larger learning gains with little extra time ([Koedinger and Aleven 2007](https://pslcdatashop.web.cmu.edu/KDDCup/FAQ/Koedinger-Aleven-EPR-07.pdf)). *For the app:* track separate skills (each card notation concept, the joker rules, calling and timing, Charleston mechanics, hand selection, declaring) and unlock graduation by mastery of all of them, not by completing a fixed number of games. Confidence: Medium (well established in academic subjects, untested for card and tile games).

**Integrate references with the play surface.** The split-attention effect (Chandler and Sweller, 1992) says that forcing learners to combine separated sources of information adds load ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worked-example_effect)). *For the app:* show the pinned card line next to the rack, with matched tiles marked, rather than on a separate screen. In the same spirit, the designers of the Mahjong Line's Big Card chose colors that would not suggest suits and would avoid red-green confusion ([Big Card FAQ](https://themahjongline.com/pages/the-big-card-faq)). An app showing NMJL lines should not rely on color alone to tell groups apart: add labels or patterns so color-blind players can follow.

**Motivation: fun before difficulty, practice that does not count.** Sloper's "have fun before I hit'em with the hard stuff" ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html)), the practice game that "doesn't count" ([Anders Ruff](https://andersruff.com/custom-printable-parties/?p=76036)), and Gee's "pleasantly frustrating" challenges ([Gee 2004](https://www.psy.gla.ac.uk/~steve/best/gee.html)) all point the same way. Beginners lose often: Sloper notes students lose a lot early ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html)), and a reader of Swain and Salk found "actually winning a game is very hard" ([Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/51364610)). *For the app:* make the first guided match winnable on purpose, as American Mahjong Practice does by default with bots that do not declare ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1458051621)), and label it clearly as a practice setting.

## 6. How teachers judge readiness to play without help

No published rubric for "ready to play alone" turned up. Teachers signal readiness in a few consistent ways. Sloper moves students from face-up to concealed racks once they stop struggling, covers most material by lesson 3, and then stays available "to help hold their hands as they polish their skills," while admitting that some students "never graduate" ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html), 2013 and 2023). Lynn M's ten sessions step from one exposed table, to club members sitting in, to concealed hands with teacher support, and end in a tournament. Course outcome statements list the same competencies: recognize the tiles; set up and follow turn order; read the card to find and narrow promising hands; pass well in the Charleston; call and expose correctly; go for Mah Jongg; and know etiquette, leading to "a confident transition to real play" ([Santa Barbara](https://www.jewishsantabarbara.org/events/mah-jongg-beginner-class-series/); [Oklahoma State](https://artscenter.okstate.edu/adult-classes/mahjong/1774-march-learn-american-mahjong-9); [Lexington](https://lexingtoncommunityed.org/?p=74567)). Next-level classes add choosing hands efficiently, switching hands when plans change, and defense ([Boulder JCC](https://www.boulderjcc.org/events/2026/09/16/boulder-jcc-programs/mah-jongg-september-beginner-lessons-evening/)). Refresher classes for returning players review the card, the Charleston and the NMJL rules, which suggests these are the skills that fade ([OASIS refresher](https://washington-metro.oasiseverywhere.org/product/refresh-your-mah-jongg-game/)). Pace is a practical test of readiness: "Keep the game moving!" and a Charleston pace of 10 to 15 seconds ([column 375](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column375a.html); [FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html)). No standard credential exists: Sloper notes an earlier teacher certification body has disbanded ([FAQ 26](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq26.html)), and the "certification" one class offers is described as a retail affiliation badge ([Menage a Mahj](https://www.tickettailor.com/events/menageamahj/2368228)). Online, I Love Mahj's progression runs from Level 1 bots with Practice Mode on, to Level 2 bots, and it tracks think times as well as wins ([Learn page](https://ilovemahj.com/learn-american-mah-jongg)). Confidence: Medium-Low that these criteria add up to a reliable bar; they are drawn from course descriptions and teachers' remarks, not from a tested assessment.

What learning science adds: judge each skill by its own mastery estimate with a high threshold ([Koedinger and Aleven](https://pslcdatashop.web.cmu.edu/KDDCup/FAQ/Koedinger-Aleven-EPR-07.pdf)), count hints as evidence against mastery, and confirm retention after a gap ([Cepeda et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/)). Winning should not be a criterion, because the outcome of any one game is largely luck and beginners lose a lot even when they play correctly.

## 7. Recommendations for the app

### 7.1 Overall shape

Use a hybrid sequence. Run a short, card-free "fish tank" round right after the tiles (Sloper's approach, supported by Gee, Andersen and the Grogan method) so the learner feels the draw-and-discard rhythm and the 14-tile goal. Then teach the card before any game that uses it (Sandberg's point, and the majority practice), then hand selection, then the Charleston, matching the order in nearly every class reviewed (sections 1.1 and 1.2). Group the scenarios into five short rounds, mirroring the five-day Santa Barbara course and Sloper's three-lesson minimum, so a learner can finish a round in one sitting and come back later, which builds in spacing.

### 7.3 Guided practice matches (support fades across the three)

**Match 1, open table.** All four racks are visible, as in Sloper's face-up lessons and Grogan's openly played first game. Bots narrate each decision in one sentence (a worked example). The app proposes two or three target lines ranked by tiles still needed, and the learner pins one, as in Maylee, American Mahjong Practice and I Love Mahj's Practice Mode. Rule errors get immediate, explanatory one- or two-sentence feedback (Shute; Koedinger and Aleven). Rule cards appear the first time their situation arises (Andersen). Undo is available. The Charleston uses the full, partial, then solo pattern across its passes. Bots are tuned so the learner can finish a hand, clearly labeled. Strategy tips are limited to a few well-sourced ones: keep options open in the Charleston (Sloper, Mahjong 4 Friends); pairs are the hard part, so prefer hands whose pairs you already hold (Miller-Small); hands without pairs let jokers go anywhere (Sloper, column 815).

**Match 2, concealed table.** Bot racks are hidden, matching Sloper's step of concealing tiles once students stop struggling. Target suggestions appear only on request, through tiered hints. Rule errors still get immediate feedback, but strategy feedback moves to an end-of-hand review (delayed feedback for transfer, per Shute; Sloper's "between games"). Introduce reading opponents' exposures, one of I Love Mahj's three drill types, and the 10 to 15 second pace as a gentle indicator rather than a timer that punishes. Bots play at normal strength and can win.

**Match 3, dress rehearsal.** Free-play conditions with the cheat sheet the learner will keep after graduating, plus a small hint budget. Rules are enforced as at a real table: an illegal exposure produces a dead hand, explained afterward. A full post-game review follows, and the graduation check is applied (7.5).

### 7.4 Feedback and hint system

Build one engine with four tiers (nudge, family, line, answer); the last tier unlocks only after an attempt (Koedinger and Aleven, on bottom-out hints). Help appears unprompted only after repeated errors on the same skill. Each explanation stays to one or two sentences, with a "why" link to the scenario that taught it (Shute). Never compare the learner with other players (Shute). Hint use feeds the mastery model.

### 7.5 Graduation milestones

Unlock free play when all of the following hold. These criteria are synthesized from section 6 and should be treated as starting values to tune.

1. **Skill mastery.** Every tracked skill (each notation concept in section 2.2, the joker rules, calling and timing, Charleston mechanics, choosing a direction, concealed hands, declaring) passes a high mastery threshold, using the Cognitive Tutor convention of about 0.95 as a starting point, with hint use counted against it.
2. **A clean concealed game.** In Match 3, the learner plays a complete game with no illegal moves attempted, no dead hand, and no missed winning tile that the app can detect. Winning is not required.
3. **Hand selection.** By the end of the Charleston, the learner has picked a viable direction in most deals, as judged by the app's own hand ranking.
4. **Pace.** Median Charleston decision time is around 15 seconds or less, shown as advice rather than a hard gate, since it is a table custom.
5. **Retention.** A short card-reading check passed at least a day after the last guided match (Cepeda).

Learners can stay in guided mode as long as they want, with no penalty (Sloper's students who "never graduate"). After graduation, keep the cheat sheet, offer occasional spaced refresher puzzles, and add a "new card" module each spring. A new card arrives every year, books keyed to old cards go stale ([FAQ 3](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/mjfaq03.html)), and playing last year's hand is a real error Sloper has documented ([column 809](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column2/column809.html)). Keep card-specific scenarios separate from rules that never change, so the annual update is cheap.

### 7.6 Content guardrails

Encode only official rules, and verify every rule detail against *Mah Jongg Made Easy*, especially exposure correction, dead-hand procedure and payments (section 2.4). Label any teaching simplification, such as the card-free first game, on screen as temporary. Do not teach house rules (hot wall, the "same" rule for joker discards, penalties) except as "you may meet this at some tables" notes. Run all tutorial text through the misconception list in section 2.7 before release.

## 8. Open questions and weak spots

The web-search budget ran out near the end of this pass, so the following could not be closed:

- **What NMJL Online's tutorial covers** is not documented anywhere I could reach. Playing the free tutorial would settle it.
- **The NMJL rulebook's contents and order.** *Mah Jongg Made Easy* was not available online. Several fine points rest on secondary sources: exposure correction, the full dead-hand procedure, and how payments stack.
- **A published readiness rubric.** None was found. The graduation criteria are a synthesis and should be tested with real learners.
- **Video courses with transcripts.** None were analyzed. The class sequences come from written lesson plans, course listings, books and teacher columns.
- **Naming stories.** Only a few name meanings are sourced here (Bams, Craks, Dots, the bird on the 1 Bam, the "Soap" nickname). The reasons behind "Soap," "Charleston," "pung" and "kong" need a sourced history pass.
- **Opposite dragons.** Definitions vary in wording, and the 2026 card's own notes should be the final authority for each line.
- **Learning science is borrowed.** The tutor and feedback research comes from math, programming and science learning, and the tutorial study used other games. Applying it to mah jongg is reasonable but untested.
- **Weak or commercial sources.** App listings, class pages and brand blogs (Mahjong 4 Friends, Miss Mahjong, Anders Ruff, mahjongg.ai.studio) are useful evidence of current practice but not authorities. One beginner-mistakes article was set aside because its blind-pass description conflicts with stronger sources (section 2.7).
